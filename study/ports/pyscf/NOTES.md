# P0: lib/hf against PySCF 2.5.0

The first port test. The Viv side is lib/hf's one
entry path, `scf_with(mol, o)` -> `integrals(mol, o)` -> `scf(setup)` ->
`scf_iterate`; the original is PySCF 2.5.0 as installed (upstream/SOURCES.md).
Nothing of PySCF is copied: PySCF is the oracle and the speed reference.

## The cases

Water (the geometry of tools/pyscf_reference.py) in STO-3G, 6-31G, cc-pVDZ
and cc-pVTZ; benzene in cc-pVDZ (test_bz.py's geometry); (H2O)n, n = 1, 2,
4, 8, in 6-31G* (the water copied to the corners of a cube with 3.0 A
edges); the water cation (doublet) in 6-31G and cc-pVDZ by UHF. Spherical
functions throughout (PySCF's default; Viv's `cart: 0`). Every basis set
and angular momentum asked for runs, up to f (cc-pVTZ oxygen).

## Differences from PySCF, routine by routine

- **Guess.** Both start from the core Hamiltonian (PySCF `init_guess =
  "1e"`, Viv `GS_CORE`). PySCF's default guess, `minao`, needs atomic
  densities lib/hf does not carry (FRICTION pyscf-004).
- **DIIS.** CDIIS over 8 matrices on both sides. PySCF leaves the guess's
  Fock matrix out of the history (`diis_start_cycle = 1`); lib/hf put it in.
  The difference decides which state UHF lands on: from the core guess on the
  water cation, with the guess's matrix in the history, both programs
  converge to a state 0.0721 Eh above the one PySCF reports by default
  (PySCF with `diis_start_cycle = 0` gives -75.50839436719134, Viv's old
  default -75.50839436719124). This port added `ScfOptions.diis_start`, and
  the hf-fast branch made 1 its default, as in PySCF; the generated programs
  still pass `diis_start: 1` explicitly. The error vectors are the same up to an orthogonal change
  of basis: PySCF's `C^T (S D F - F D S) C` in the orbitals of the first
  Fock matrix, Viv's `F' P' - P' F'` in the basis of canonical
  orthogonalization; the DIIS coefficients depend only on their inner
  products.
- **Convergence test.** PySCF: `|dE| < conv_tol` and the Frobenius norm of
  the occupied-virtual gradient below `conv_tol_grad`, then one more cycle
  (`conv_check`). Viv: `|dE| < e_tol` and the largest commutator element
  below `grad_tol`, tested after the J/K build. So the iteration counts are
  not the same quantity: PySCF builds J and K cycles + 2 times (the guess's,
  one per cycle, the check), Viv once per iteration. The tables give both and
  the time per J/K build.
- **Diagonalization.** PySCF calls `scipy.linalg.eigh(F, S)` (LAPACK,
  generalized) every iteration. Viv orthogonalizes once (canonical,
  `lindep` 1e-8) and diagonalizes `X^T F X` with its own Householder
  tridiagonalization and QL iteration.
- **Integrals.** PySCF: libcint (generated C, Rys quadrature) computes
  spherical integrals shell quartet by shell quartet, and computes the
  primitive integrals of a general contraction once for all its
  contractions. Viv: McMurchie-Davidson blocks (lib/hf/md_eri) with Schwarz
  screening at 1e-14. Since hf-fast, the shells of one general contraction
  (same atom, l and exponents; carbon's two s contractions in cc-pVDZ) form
  one group whose primitive integrals are computed once, and a spherical
  basis gets each block moved to spherical functions as it is computed
  (`mderi_sph`). Before, every segmented shell was its own pair and the
  whole Cartesian array was built first, then transformed (lib/hf/transform,
  still the check: `tr4_two_pass`).
- **J and K.** In core: both contract the packed 8-fold array (libcvhf
  `CVHFnrs8_incore_drv`; Viv `jk_packed`). PySCF's `get_veff` builds the
  potential incrementally from `D - D_last`; with the integrals in core that
  changes nothing but the rounding. Direct (benzene, (H2O)8): both screen
  shell quartets by the Schwarz bound and by the density with PySCF's rule
  and threshold (`CVHFnrs8_prescreen`, `direct_scf_tol = 1e-13`: skip when
  `Q_ab Q_cd max(4 D_ab, 4 D_cd, D_ac, D_ad, D_bc, D_bd)` is at or below it),
  and both build J and K from the change of density since the last build
  (Viv `jk_update`, lib/hf/jk). Viv also builds from the whole density every
  `fock_rebuild` = 8 iterations, and on every iteration from the one whose
  incremental build first meets the convergence test, so the energy it
  returns comes from a full build; PySCF 2.5.0 does neither (its
  `conv_check` cycle is incremental too). The switch is needed at the
  verification settings: the incremental builds of (H2O)8 carry about 1e-11
  Eh of noise (each skipped quartet's terms are below 1e-13, and late in the
  SCF most quartets of the change of density are skipped), which is ten
  times e_tol; staying incremental took 53 iterations to meet the test by
  chance, full builds from there converge in a few. The density screening keeps most
  quartets of these two molecules even late in the SCF (measured below), so
  the per-build gap is the integral code's speed, not the screening.
- **Why direct for benzene and (H2O)8.** Their packed ERIs (21.1 and 54.5
  million values) are over the 16,000,000-element array limit of every Viv
  engine, so Viv can only run them direct; PySCF holds them in core by
  default. The timing compares direct with direct and also reports PySCF in
  core (FRICTION pyscf-002).
- **Checkpoint.** PySCF writes an HDF5 checkpoint every cycle by default.
  The timed PySCF column turns it off (`mf.chkfile = None`), since Viv
  writes none; the `chk` column keeps it.

## Tolerances (decided before the comparison ran)

- Energy: 1e-10 Eh at the verification settings (PySCF conv_tol 1e-12,
  conv_tol_grad 1e-8; Viv e_tol 1e-12, grad_tol 1e-9). Both energies are
  converged to about 1e-12 and both use the same integrals to about 1e-13
  relative, so 1e-10 leaves two orders of margin. PySCF's own tests assert 9
  places.
- Orbital energies: 1e-8 Eh. They are first order in the density's error,
  which conv_tol_grad 1e-8 bounds.
- A timing run (PySCF conv_tol 1e-10 with its default gradient threshold
  1e-5; Viv e_tol 1e-10, grad_tol 1e-5) must land within 1e-8 Eh of the
  verification energy, or its time does not count.

## Engines

Parity cases (water STO-3G and 6-31G, the cation 6-31G UHF) and pzcheck run
in the interpreter, native C and wasm and print the same bytes
(tools/three-engines.sh, in check.sh). Every other case is checked natively
only: the interpreter would take minutes to hours, and the stage clocks exist
only in the C build. wasm is timed for the cases that fit its time budget;
(H2O)8 is native only.

## Timing method

See results/RESULTS.md for the tables and the loads they were taken at.

- PySCF: in process, a fresh `scf.RHF(mol)` per run so the integrals are
  computed each time, as Viv computes them; one warm-up run; the median of
  the rest. Stages by wrapping the PySCF methods that do them (no change to
  PySCF): `Mole.intor` by integral name, `get_hcore`, `get_ovlp`,
  `get_init_guess`, `get_veff`/`get_jk`, `CDIIS.update`, `eig`,
  `energy_tot`/`energy_elec`, and apart from those `get_fock`, `make_rdm1`,
  `get_grad`, `get_occ`, `dump_chk`. Exclusive times.
- Viv: there is no clock builtin, so the harness hooks the generated C's
  step events (`viv_step`, called at every `step` boundary in the default
  and the release build): `port.py` inserts a clock before the function and
  prints the exclusive time per `def/step` at exit. No other line of the
  program changes, and the steps are lib/hf's own (`integrals/one_electron`,
  `ao_eri/compute`, `scf_iterate/coulomb_exchange`, ...). The SCF time is
  the sum of `scf_with`'s two steps, which is what PySCF's `kernel()` plus
  its integrals cover. wasm and the interpreter have no hook: their time is
  the process time minus the process time of `<case>_0.viv`, which builds the
  molecule and stops.
- One thread: OMP_NUM_THREADS, OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS and the rest of the
  benchmark README's list set to 1; PySCF's `lib.num_threads(1)` (its C is
  built without OpenMP in any case); the CPU/wall ratio is recorded.
- Final numbers only with the 1-minute load average below 2 through the
  whole run; anything else is provisional and marked `*`.

The Viv SCF driver includes its own symmetric eigensolver (tred2 + tql2, about 230 lines), which
PySCF takes from LAPACK. The gate (1.5x for NumPy-style code) is met for both Python parts. A
reader who wants the integrals must read libcint's generated C on PySCF's side and about
993 lines of Viv on this side.

Intent on the path: 160 of 160 defs have a `what`; 4 entries are
stale or unstamped (c2s_basis, c2s_block, c2s_closed, eig_sort); the steps of the path's modules are
explained, 24 of 24.

## Where Viv's time goes, and what would recover it

hold 85 to 99.7% of Viv's time, the two-electron integrals and the J/K
contraction, and both are lib/hf's own Viv against PySCF's C.
